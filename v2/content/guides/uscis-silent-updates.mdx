This guide is for anyone waiting on a USCIS case (an I-485, I-140, I-765 or any other form) who has heard about "silent updates", "API movement" or codes such as FTA0 and wants to know what they mean. To see how long USCIS usually takes on your form, check PERM Tracker's [USCIS processing times page](/uscis-processing-times), which shows USCIS's quarterly median for every form beside its receipts and pending counts.

<Callout type="important" title="The short answer">
A silent update is a change in your own USCIS account's case data (a new date or a new code) while the status on USCIS's public page stays the same. USCIS says some of these changes are "internal or administrative updates that do not impact the outcome of a case." So a silent update tells you something happened. It doesn't tell you what, and it doesn't mean a decision is near.
</Callout>

## Where a case's status shows up

A case's status shows up in three places: Case Status Online, your USCIS online account, and USCIS's Case Status API for approved apps. Silent updates show up in your online account.

The first two are USCIS's own tools: [Case Status Online](https://egov.uscis.gov/casestatus/landing.do) and [your online account](https://my.uscis.gov). USCIS's [page on checking your status](https://www.uscis.gov/tools/checking-your-case-status-online) says the account lets you "receive your most recent case updates including up to the last five actions on your case."

## What USCIS says causes them

USCIS's own description of its [Case Status API](https://developer.uscis.gov/api/case-status) says most plainly what moves behind a case. Its modified date "is updated when an event occurs related to a receiptNumber." Some of those events show up as a new status or a new history entry. Others don't: "Internal or administrative updates that do not impact the outcome of a case may also cause a modifiedDate update."

That sentence is the best guide to a silent update. Something was done on the file. It may be a step toward a decision, or it may be a file moving between offices, a record being touched, or a routine check.

## FTA0, IAF, FJ, FTA1 and H008: what the codes are

People who look at the case data their own signed-in account loads see short codes beside dates. USCIS has never published a key to the codes in your account. Three of the common ones do appear in older official code lists, under plain names.

The manual is [a scan USCIS released under the Freedom of Information Act](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/foia/CLAIMS_3_Database_User_Manual.pdf), and its code table is Attachment C. The data standard is [NIEM's list of benefit document statuses](https://niem.github.io/model/5.0/scr/BenefitDocumentStatusCategoryCodeSimpleType/), in that standard's section for screening data.

One caution goes with every row. USCIS has never said the codes in your account are the ones in that old table. The letters match. A [DHS privacy filing](https://www.dhs.gov/sites/default/files/2024-07/24_0731_priv_pia-uscis-071a-myuscisaccountexperience-appendixb-update.pdf) says the CLAIMS 3 system updates your account at "each step in the case management process", and the steps it lists include automated background checks. The record stops there.

"Database checks received" also says very little. It doesn't say which check came back, what it found or why it ran.

### The third FTA0

The best-known claim online is that a third FTA0 means an approval is a day away. Here's what stands behind it.

- **One count exists.** [One filer tallied 138 approved cases](https://www.reddit.com/r/USCIS/comments/1vov84z/) from early 2026. About half (70) were approved the day after a third FTA0. The count holds approved cases only, so it leaves out everyone who got a third FTA0 and kept waiting.
- **Many people did keep waiting.** Filers report one month, two months and longer after a third, fourth or fifth FTA0.
- **It isn't a visa number being set aside.** USCIS's [questions and answers on employment-based adjustment](https://www.uscis.gov/green-card/green-card-processes-and-procedures/fiscal-year-2023-employment-based-adjustment-of-status-faqs) say there's "no reservation or pre-allocation of a visa number" before an application is approved.
- **One day it landed on many cases at once.** On August 13, 2026, filers saw an FTA0 land on many unrelated cases in one afternoon. That fits a batch of checks coming back. It doesn't fit an officer opening one file.

No code is a decision. The decision is the status USCIS shows and the notice it mails.

## Why no tracker can show them from a receipt number

A receipt number reaches only the public data, and the codes behind silent updates live in your USCIS account, behind a login that's yours alone.

- **A receipt number reaches only the public data.** A tracker that has your number can read what Case Status Online shows, or what USCIS's API returns. Neither carries your account's codes.
- **The API's one signal is the modified date**, and USCIS doesn't send it at all for receipts that start with IOE (cases filed online): the date is "Not supported where receiptNumber prefix = IOE."
- **The codes live behind your login, and your login is yours alone.** USCIS's [account terms](https://myaccount.uscis.gov/legal-terms) bar account holders from sharing their password "with anyone else, or any entity or company, for their own use," from letting another person or company "log into, access, or use" their account, and from "Utilizing unauthorized third-party software within the USCIS online electronic immigration system."

So an app that promises silent-update codes from your receipt number alone is promising something USCIS doesn't give it. An app that asks for your USCIS login is asking you to break your account's terms.

## When you see one

Go by the status and the notices USCIS mails, which are the record, and by USCIS's posted processing time.

<StepByStep>
<Step number={1} title="Read the status and the notices">
The status on USCIS's page and the notices USCIS mails are the record. A new code beside an unchanged status is just that.
</Step>
<Step number={2} title="Compare your wait with USCIS's posted time">
USCIS posts processing times by form and office. The [guide to USCIS processing times](/guides/uscis-processing-times-median-vs-80-percent) explains what the posted figure measures.
</Step>
<Step number={3} title="Use USCIS's own channels if you're past it">
If your case is outside the posted time, USCIS's case inquiry tools and the Contact Center at 1-800-375-5283 are the route. A silent update doesn't change that.
</Step>
<Step number={4} title="Keep your login to yourself">
No tracker, forum helper or app needs your USCIS password.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## What this can't tell you

- **What a code means for your case.** Two older official lists name some of the codes. USCIS hasn't said what a code in your account means today.
- **Whether a silent update means a decision is coming.** USCIS says some don't affect the outcome at all.
- **Anything about a receipt that starts with IOE through USCIS's API.** USCIS doesn't send the modified date for those.
