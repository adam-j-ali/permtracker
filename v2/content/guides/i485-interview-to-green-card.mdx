For an employment-based I-485, the interview is usually the last big step. Then comes the wait for the decision. PERM Tracker's [green card timelines](/green-card-timelines) page measures it from every case people report about themselves, in Telegram groups, public community sheets, Reddit, VisaStats and on PERM Tracker itself, each person counted once and updated every hour.

## What the interview covers

An employment-based I-485 interview is usually short. The officer confirms who you are, goes through your answers on the I-485 with you under oath, and checks your documents against the file: your passport and visa history, the employer's letter confirming the job, and the medical exam if it isn't already in. Bring the originals of anything you sent copies of, and the documents the interview notice lists.

It ends one of three ways. Many people report an approval on the spot. Others get a request for more evidence, which pauses the case until they answer. And some leave with no decision that day, while the case waits for a supervisor or a final check. The days on this page include all three.

## Why it can take longer

**The visa bulletin.** USCIS can approve an employment-based I-485 only when the priority date is current under the [visa bulletin](/visa-bulletin)'s final action dates. A case interviewed while its date is current can still wait if the date moves back before the decision.

**A request for evidence.** The officer can ask for more documents after the interview. The approval then waits for the answer and its review.

**The job offer.** An employment-based I-485 rests on the job the PERM and I-140 were filed for. Officers commonly ask for a letter from the employer confirming the job is still offered.

**The medical exam.** The [I-693 medical exam](/guides/i693-medical-exam) has to be current at the decision. An expired one has to be done again.

## Before the interview

For the people who had one, the interview came <ReportedMedianNow step="i485-interview" /> after the I-485 was filed. ## What these figures can't tell you

They come from people who chose to report their case, so they aren't a sample of everyone, and nobody can check a date people report after PERM. People approved on the spot are likelier to say so than people still waiting, which pulls the median down. Read it as what the people who reported saw. If you've had your interview, add your dates on the [green card timelines](/green-card-timelines) page: it takes a minute, needs no account and makes the next person's figure sharper.
