A USCIS case status is the short title USCIS shows for one receipt number, with a paragraph under it. USCIS says its status tool shows "the last action taken on your case" and the next steps, if there are any ([Checking Your Case Status Online](https://www.uscis.gov/tools/checking-your-case-status-online), last reviewed August 25, 2020). Type your receipt number into PERM Tracker's free [USCIS case status page](/uscis-case-status), which also tells a DOL case number from a USCIS receipt and sends each one to the agency that holds it.

This guide explains how statuses work. To find your exact wording among every status USCIS shows, use [what each USCIS case status means](/uscis-case-statuses).

<Callout type="important" title="The short answer">
Almost every status falls into one of five groups, named by what happens next:

- USCIS has your filing
- USCIS wants something from you
- USCIS is working on it
- USCIS has decided
- Your card is on its way

Only the second group has a deadline you have to meet. Act on that one the day it appears.
</Callout>

## Where the words come from

The words come from USCIS's own system, and each one is about a single form. USCIS's developer documentation for its Case Status API shows the shape: a short title such as "Case Was Approved", a paragraph that names the form and the date, and a dated history of earlier steps ([developer.uscis.gov](https://developer.uscis.gov/api/case-status), read November 25, 2026).

USCIS publishes no list of these titles. No USCIS page read for this guide defines them either. Three appear word for word on USCIS's own pages: "Case Was Approved" in that developer documentation, and "Case Remains Pending" and "Case Was Updated to Show Fingerprints Were Taken" in its [employment-based adjustment FAQs](https://www.uscis.gov/green-card/green-card-processes-and-procedures/fiscal-year-2023-employment-based-adjustment-of-status-faqs) (last reviewed January 24, 2025). The other titles here are the ones people quote from the status page. For each, this guide explains the step behind it from the USCIS page that describes that step.

One receipt number covers one form. An I-140 and an I-485 filed together have a status each, so "approved" on one says nothing about the other. [How to read a USCIS receipt number](/guides/uscis-receipt-number) shows where each number sits on its notice.

## The statuses at a glance

Find your status and its group, and the last column says whether anything is on you. Two of them set a deadline for you.

| Status people see | Group | Is anything on you? |
|---|---|---|
| Case Was Received | USCIS has your filing | No. Keep the receipt notice. |
| Case Was Updated to Show Fingerprints Were Taken | USCIS has your filing | No. |
| Request for Evidence Was Sent | USCIS wants something | Yes. Answer by the date on the notice. |
| Interview Was Scheduled | USCIS wants something | Yes. Go, or ask to reschedule before the day. |
| Case Is Being Actively Reviewed | USCIS is working | No. |
| Case Remains Pending | USCIS is working | No. It waits on a visa number. |
| Case Was Approved | Decided | Read the approval notice. |
| Case Was Denied | Decided | Read the notice. Its deadlines run from the decision. |
| Card Was Mailed | Your card | Watch the mail and the tracking number. |

## Statuses that mean USCIS has your filing

"Case Was Received" means USCIS accepted the filing and gave it a receipt number. Nothing is asked of you yet. The paper version is Form I-797C, which USCIS uses for receipts, rejections, transfers of files, fingerprint and interview appointments, and reopened cases ([Form I-797 types and functions](https://www.uscis.gov/forms/filing-guidance/form-i-797-types-and-functions), last reviewed January 24, 2025).

If no receipt comes, USCIS draws a line. Its page says to tell it when you mailed a filing to a service center or the National Benefits Center more than 30 days ago and haven't had a receipt ([While My Case is Pending](https://www.uscis.gov/tools/while-my-case-is-pending), last reviewed October 30, 2025).

Some forms need your fingerprints, a photo or a signature. For those, USCIS books an appointment at a local Application Support Center and mails an I-797C with the date, time and place ([Preparing for Your Biometric Services Appointment](https://www.uscis.gov/forms/filing-guidance/preparing-for-your-biometric-services-appointment), read November 25, 2026).

"Case Was Updated to Show Fingerprints Were Taken" records that step. USCIS has explained one odd case of it. Over 100,000 adjustment applicants who'd given biometrics long before saw this status appear in October and November 2022, because of an internal update to its systems. USCIS said those cases carry on as usual (the employment-based adjustment FAQs above, answer updated December 8, 2023).

A case can also move to another office. The I-797C is the notice for that too. An archived USCIS page adds that the receipt number stays the same after a transfer ([Workload Transfer Updates](https://www.uscis.gov/archive/workload-transfer-updates), archived, last reviewed December 18, 2023).

## Statuses that ask something of you

"Request for Evidence Was Sent" and "Interview Was Scheduled" are the two statuses with a deadline for you. Read the notice the day it arrives, and give it to your attorney if you have one.

A request for evidence comes on Form I-797E, the notice USCIS issues "to request evidence" (the I-797 page above). The rules for it are in the regulation, 8 CFR 103.2(b), read on [eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-103/section-103.2) on November 25, 2026.

- The notice states its own deadline. It can't be more than twelve weeks away for a request for evidence, or more than thirty days for a notice of intent to deny, and USCIS can't grant more time on either (paragraph (b)(8)(iv)).
- Everything goes back together, along with the original notice. Sending only part of it counts as asking USCIS to decide on what it already has (paragraph (b)(11)).
- If no answer arrives by the deadline, USCIS may deny the case as abandoned, on the record, or both (paragraph (b)(13)(i)).
- The request pauses any time limit USCIS works under. The count picks up where it stopped once USCIS receives your answer (paragraph (b)(10)(i)). On a premium-processed petition this matters a lot, and [the I-140 premium processing guide](/guides/i140-processing-times-and-premium) explains how its clock restarts.

An interview notice is an I-797C too. USCIS reschedules only for emergencies outside your control. It asks you to call well before the interview date, and it says some interviews can't be moved at all (While My Case is Pending, above). Missing one can end the case. The regulation treats a missed interview or biometrics appointment as abandoned, unless USCIS received a change of address or a rescheduling request by the appointment time (8 CFR 103.2(b)(13)(ii)). A biometrics appointment is rescheduled only through your USCIS online account, before the appointment, and for good cause (the biometrics page above).

## Statuses that mean USCIS is working on it

"Case Is Being Actively Reviewed" and "Case Remains Pending" both mean USCIS has the case and has nothing to ask you yet. Neither one is a decision.

USCIS doesn't define "Case Is Being Actively Reviewed" on any page read for this guide. So it says only that the case is open and with USCIS. Nothing USCIS has published says it means a decision is near.

"Case Remains Pending" has a definition. USCIS says it means an officer reviewed the application and couldn't approve it that day because the State Department couldn't allocate a visa number. Processing resumes once a number can be allocated, and USCIS adds that not every later action changes the message you see (the employment-based adjustment FAQs, answer updated March 22, 2023). For an I-485, that number depends on the [visa bulletin](/visa-bulletin) and your priority date.

Quiet changes happen too. USCIS's developer documentation says "internal or administrative updates" that don't affect a case's outcome can still change its record. Your online account may show those as dated codes while the public status stays the same. [USCIS silent updates](/guides/uscis-silent-updates) covers what's known about those codes, and PERM Tracker's [account update reader](/uscis-account-reader) labels each one with where its reading comes from.

While a case is in this group, USCIS won't look into it early. Its page says it doesn't research cases that are within the expected processing times (While My Case is Pending). The [USCIS case inquiry date tool](/tools/uscis-case-inquiry) takes your form, office and receipt date and shows the first day USCIS takes a question about your case.

## Statuses that mean a decision

"Case Was Approved" and "Case Was Denied" are decisions. In both cases a notice follows, and that notice is the document that counts. USCIS's own example of the approved paragraph says it approved the form, sent an approval notice and asks you to follow the instructions in it (developer documentation above).

What follows an approval depends on the form. After an I-140 is approved for someone living abroad, USCIS sends the petition to the State Department's National Visa Center ([Consular Processing](https://www.uscis.gov/green-card/green-card-processes-and-procedures/consular-processing), last reviewed July 20, 2023). [NVC and the DS-260](/guides/nvc-and-ds-260-after-an-approved-i140) walks through the steps from there. Inside the US, the green card itself comes through the I-485, and [the I-485 timeline](/guides/i485-timeline-each-step) covers that.

If the status says an approval notice was mailed more than 30 days ago and none arrived, USCIS asks for an e-Request ([After Receiving a Decision](https://www.uscis.gov/green-card/after-receiving-a-decision), last reviewed February 27, 2023).

A denial notice explains why. It also says whether you can appeal, and how. With some exceptions, you can ask USCIS to reopen or reconsider instead (the same page). The deadlines are short and they run from the decision, so act early:

- An appeal on Form I-290B goes in within 30 days after the decision is served (8 CFR 103.3(a)(2)(i)).
- A motion to reopen or reconsider goes in within 30 days of the decision (8 CFR 103.5(a)(1)(i)).
- When the decision came by mail, 3 days are added to the period (8 CFR 103.8(b)).

A rejection is a different thing from a denial. A rejected filing doesn't keep its filing date, and a rejection can't be appealed (8 CFR 103.2(a)(7)(ii) and (iii)). The regulation lists the grounds, among them a missing valid signature and the wrong fee. The fix is to file again.

## Statuses about your card

The card statuses follow an approved green card or work permit application, and they end with a package in your mailbox. The I-797D is the notice that travels with a card (the I-797 page above).

USCIS mails cards by U.S. Postal Service Priority Mail with tracking. Your USCIS online account shows the tracking number once the card is mailed. Without an account, Case Status Online shows whether USCIS has mailed it ([How to Track Delivery of Your Notice or Card](https://www.uscis.gov/forms/filing-guidance/how-to-track-delivery-of-your-notice-or-secure-identity-document-or-card), last reviewed October 11, 2024).

If tracking says delivered and nothing came, start with the Postal Service's missing mail steps. If that fails, contact USCIS or file a case inquiry for a card that didn't arrive (same page). For a new permanent resident, USCIS sends a welcome notice and then the card. It asks you to file an e-Request when 30 days pass after the welcome notice with no card (After Receiving a Decision).

## What this can't tell you

- When your own case moves. A status describes the last step. It doesn't forecast the next one.
- The exact wording on your screen. USCIS doesn't publish its titles, so a title can be worded a little differently from the ones quoted here.
- What a code in your online account means. That's in [the silent updates guide](/guides/uscis-silent-updates).
- Anything about a DOL case. PERM and prevailing wage statuses come from DOL, and [what PERM case statuses mean](/guides/what-perm-case-statuses-mean) covers them.
