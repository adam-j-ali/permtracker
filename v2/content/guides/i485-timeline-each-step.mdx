An employment-based I-485 moves through the same handful of steps for almost everyone, and the question is always how long each one takes right now. PERM Tracker's [green card timelines](/green-card-timelines) page answers it from every case people report about themselves, in Telegram groups, public community sheets, Reddit, VisaStats and on PERM Tracker itself, each person counted once and updated every hour.

## Each step, in order

**Biometrics.** USCIS mails an appointment at an Application Support Center to take fingerprints and a photo. The appointment came <ReportedMedianNow step="i485-bio" /> after filing.

**The work permit.** An I-765 filed with the I-485 asks for a work permit on the pending application, category (c)(9). It was approved <ReportedMedianNow step="i485-ead" /> after filing. The [EAD and advance parole guide](/guides/ead-and-advance-parole-timelines) has USCIS's own figures beside it.

**The travel document.** An I-131 filed with the I-485 asks for advance parole, so you can travel and come back while the I-485 waits. It came <ReportedMedianNow step="i485-ap" /> after filing.

**The move to a field office.** Many employment-based I-485s start at the National Benefits Center and are later sent to a local field office, which is the office that would interview you. Your online case status then says the case was transferred and a new office has jurisdiction. The move came <ReportedMedianNow step="i485-office" /> after filing. Each [field office's page](/i485-by-field-office) shows its own cases, and some offices are much faster than others.

**The interview.** Many employment-based cases are approved without one. For the people who had one, it came <ReportedMedianNow step="i485-interview" /> after filing, and the approval followed <ReportedMedianNow step="interview-gc" /> later. The [interview guide](/guides/i485-interview-to-green-card) covers that stretch in detail.

**The card.** After approval, your case status says the card is being produced, then that it was mailed. It reached people <ReportedMedianNow step="gc-card" /> after the approval.

## Where you stand

Your own place depends on your office and on the [visa bulletin](/visa-bulletin), which decides whether an I-485 can be filed and approved at all. USCIS's [I-485 processing times](/uscis-processing-times/i-485) are its own medians across every case, and the [I-485 inventory](/guides/i485-inventory-month-by-month) counts how many employment-based I-485s are pending ahead of each priority date.

If you're waiting on one, add your dates on the [green card timelines](/green-card-timelines) page. It takes a minute with no account and no case number, and every new timeline makes these figures sharper for the next person.

## What these figures can't tell you

They come from people who chose to report their case, so they aren't a sample of everyone. People with a fast or a stuck case are likelier to post than people whose case went as expected. Nobody can check a date people report after PERM, and PERM Tracker doesn't. Read each median as what the people who reported saw, beside USCIS's own figures, never in place of them.
