Three statuses cover a PERM under appeal, and they're steps on one ladder rather than alternatives. Reconsideration asks the officer who denied the case to change their mind. A request for review asks a judge to. BALCA APPEALS is the case sitting with those judges. Each step has its own rule, its own window, and a measurable population of cases waiting at it. PERM Tracker's [RFI, audit and appeal page](/perm-rfi-audit) counts the cases at each step from its daily read of DOL's own case-status service.

## The ladder

The three appeal statuses are steps on one ladder: reconsideration by the same officer, a request for review, then the case at BALCA. The first two each have a 30-day window.

## How each step has ended so far

Almost every denial goes back to the officer first. Our daily check reads DOL's status for every PERM case, so each move from one step to the next is on record. This is the live count, the same one the [RFI, audit and appeal page](/perm-rfi-audit) shows.

The rows read as one path. A denial almost always goes back to the officer. A request the officer refuses usually gets a NORD, and most NORDs end in a denial. Almost every request for review goes on to the Board, which is how a denial reaches it.

Two limits on these figures. They count only the moves our check watched, so they'll shift as the record grows. And no figure here forecasts one case. The [NORD guide](/guides/perm-nord-issued-meaning) covers what that status means.

## Reconsideration

The request goes to the officer who issued the denial, within 30 days of it. It can only carry documents DOL already received, or documents the employer had no earlier chance to submit that existed when the application was filed and were kept on file. That's the whole of what can be added, and it's why reconsideration works for a misread form and rarely for a missing one.

The officer has two choices: reconsider, or treat the request as a request for review and send it to the Board. Under 656.24(g)(3) reconsideration isn't available where the denial came from disregarding a system prompt or a direct instruction.

The employer picks one route and says which in writing. DOL's own answers on appeals say a request that's vague or asks for both is handled as reconsideration. If the officer upholds the denial, DOL sends a notice. The employer then has 30 days from that notice to ask for Board review.

## Request for review

This is the step that puts the denial in front of the Board. It goes to the officer rather than to BALCA directly, within 30 days of the determination. It has to identify the determination, set out the grounds and attach the final determination. The officer forwards the file. Nothing new is added to the record at this point either.

Letting both this window and the reconsideration window pass counts as a failure to exhaust administrative remedies. The denial then becomes the final determination of the Secretary, which forecloses a federal court looking at it later.

## BALCA

The Board of Alien Labor Certification Appeals is a panel of administrative law judges sitting outside the office that denied the case. It reviews the record the decision was made on, plus the request for review and any briefs, and every party gets 30 days to file a statement of position. Then the Board reaches one of the three outcomes above. Because the review is on the record, the argument is legal: whether the officer applied the regulation correctly to the facts already in the file. Improving the facts isn't on the table.

A fourth status, DENIED - BALCA DISMISSED, marks an appeal that ended without the Board ruling on its merits. DOL publishes no definition, so the words are the record.

## What this guide can't tell you

It can't tell you whether your denial is worth appealing, how long BALCA will take, or the chance for your case.

- Whether your denial has grounds worth appealing. That's the attorney's reading of the letter against the file.
- How long BALCA will take. No published time exists. The cases waiting are what can be measured.
- The chance for your case. The shares above describe everyone's motions over a few weeks. They don't weigh the grounds in one file. BALCA's own decisions are published, but this site doesn't hold them as data yet.
