A USCIS receipt number looks like `IOE0912345678`. It's the one piece of text every later notice, every case status lookup and every call to USCIS keys on, and it's easy to confuse with the DOL case number that came before it. Type either into PERM Tracker's free [receipt number lookup](/uscis-case-status) and it says which one you have.

## What USCIS says it is

USCIS defines the term in its own glossary, at https://www.uscis.gov/tools/glossary: "The receipt number is a unique 13-character identifier USCIS provides for each application or petition it receives. We use it to identify and track our cases. The receipt number consists of 3 letters, such as EAC, WAC, LIN, SRC, NBC, MSC or IOE, with 10 numbers. You can find it on notices of action USCIS has sent you."

Three parts of that definition matter here.

- **One per application or petition.** An I-140, an I-485, an I-765 and an I-131 filed together get a receipt number each, four in all. When someone tells you "your case was approved", the first question is which number they were reading.
- **Three letters, then ten digits, no hyphens.** `IOE0912345678` is a receipt number. `G-100-26125-868956` isn't. That's a DOL PERM number, and it's explained in [the PERM case number format guide](/guides/perm-case-number-format).
- **It's on the notice.** Nobody has to derive it. If you don't have a notice, whoever filed the form has one.

## The three letters

USCIS names seven prefixes in the glossary and doesn't say what each stands for on that page. `IOE` is the one worth knowing. The I-140 form page (https://www.uscis.gov/i-140) describes an online-filed petition as a "pending Form I-140 (with a receipt number that begins with IOE)". So an `IOE` number is a filing that went through the USCIS online system rather than the mail. The other six are the codes you'll see on mailed filings. USCIS's glossary lists them without mapping them to offices, and this guide won't map them either.

## The ten digits

You'll read in a lot of places that the ten digits encode the fiscal year, a computer workday and a sequence number. USCIS's glossary doesn't say so, and no USCIS page this site has read publishes a key. Treat that decoding as folklore. It may be right, but it isn't documented. Nothing about your case depends on it. The number is an identifier, and the only thing you need to do is copy it exactly.

## Where it sits on the notice

Every USCIS notice is a Form I-797, and USCIS keeps a page describing the variants at https://www.uscis.gov/forms/filing-guidance/form-i-797-types-and-functions (last reviewed Jan 24, 2025). Form I-797 "is NOT a form you can fill out". USCIS issues it "to communicate with applicants/petitioners or convey an immigration benefit."

| Notice | What USCIS says it's for |
|---|---|
| I-797, Notice of Action | "Issued to communicate receipt or approval of an application or petition." |
| I-797A | "Issued to an applicant as a replacement Form I-94." |
| I-797B | "Issued for approval of an alien worker petition." |
| I-797C | "Issued to communicate receipt or rejection of an applications or petitions; transfer of files; fingerprint biometric, interview and re-scheduled appointments; additional actions, and re-opening of cases." |
| I-797D | "Accompanies benefit cards." |
| I-797E | "Issued to request evidence." |
| I-797F | "Transportation Letter. Issued overseas to allow applicants to travel." |

The receipt notice you get first is an I-797C. USCIS's glossary says only that the number is on the notices of action, and the I-797 page doesn't describe the layout, so look for the field labeled Receipt Number. The same notice names the form it's about, and that form name is what tells you which of your filings the number belongs to. The number alone doesn't.

## How soon the notice arrives

USCIS prints one figure for a paper filing, and it's on a form. Form G-1145 is the one-page sheet that asks for a text or an email when a lockbox accepts a filing. It says "We will send the e-Notification within 24 hours after we accept your application." It goes on: "USCIS will also mail you a receipt notice (I-797C), which you will receive within 10 days after your application has been accepted" ([Form G-1145](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/forms/g-1145.pdf), edition 09/26/14). Both clocks start when USCIS accepts the filing. Its pages give no figure for the days between mailing a package and that acceptance.

Filed online, it's faster. "For most forms, you will immediately receive a receipt notice that contains your receipt number. For some forms, it may take up to 3 days to receive a receipt number." A paper copy follows by mail ([USCIS, Online Filing Options](https://www.uscis.gov/online-filing-options)).

If nothing comes, USCIS draws the line at about 30 days. Its pages don't agree on which 30.

| Where it was filed | When USCIS says to ask | Who to ask |
|---|---|---|
| A service center or the National Benefits Center | "more than 30 days ago ... and have not yet received a receipt in the mail" | The Contact Center, per [While My Case is Pending](https://www.uscis.gov/tools/while-my-case-is-pending) |
| A lockbox | "after 30 business days of processing" | lockboxsupport@uscis.dhs.gov, per [Lockbox Filing Information](https://www.uscis.gov/forms/filing-guidance/lockbox-filing-information) |

USCIS's [contact page](https://www.uscis.gov/about-us/contact-us) words the lockbox case as "Filing more than 30 days ago and not yet receiving a receipt notice." So the same wait is printed as 30 days on one page and 30 business days on another. These pages were read on November 9, 2026.

## Telling your numbers apart

If several filings went in together, keep a short table: receipt number, form, receipt date. Every later question ("was it the 485 or the 765 that got approved?") gets answered by reading the form column. [The guide to what Emma told you](/guides/what-emma-told-you) walks through the case where an agent quoted a date without naming the form.

One more distinction is easy to miss. An approval notice on the Supplement J that accompanies an employment-based I-485 is a notice about the job offer. It says nothing about the green card. That one has [its own guide](/guides/i485-vs-i485j-supplement-j).

## Checking a number

USCIS Case Status Online takes one receipt number at a time and returns a short paragraph of plain text. This site's [USCIS case status page](/uscis-case-status) does the same lookup and keeps the date it was read, so you can see whether the text changed since the last time. It reads the same USCIS system, so it knows nothing USCIS hasn't published about your case.

The notice also carries a receipt date, and that date is what times the case. The [USCIS case inquiry date tool](/tools/uscis-case-inquiry) takes it and shows when cases like yours were decided and the day USCIS takes a question about yours.

## What this can't tell you

- **Which form the number belongs to.** The notice says, and the number doesn't. Read the form name printed on the notice.
- **What the ten digits mean.** USCIS publishes no key. Anything you read about fiscal years and workdays is unverified against USCIS's own pages.
- **The status of the case.** A receipt number is a label. Status comes from a lookup, and a lookup returns whatever USCIS has chosen to publish, which is usually one sentence.
- **Anything about a DOL case.** PERM, prevailing wage and LCA numbers live at DOL. They're looked up by DOL case number on [the DOL case status page](/perm-case-status).
