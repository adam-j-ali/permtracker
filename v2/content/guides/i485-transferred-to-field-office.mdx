A transfer to a local field office is one of the most common things to happen to an employment-based I-485, and one of the least explained. PERM Tracker's [I-485 by field office](/i485-by-field-office) pages show what happened at each office, from USCIS's own quarterly counts and from every case people report about themselves in Telegram and Facebook groups, public community sheets, Reddit and on PERM Tracker itself.

<ReportedStepsNow steps="i485-office,office-gc,interview-gc" />

## What the transfer means

Many employment-based I-485s are first handled at the National Benefits Center, which does the background work without meeting you. When a case needs a local office, usually to hold an interview or finish the review near where you live, it's sent to the field office that covers your address. Your online case status then says the case was transferred and a new office has jurisdiction.

A transfer isn't a problem with the case. It's where the decision will be made.

## Which office you get

The office is the one that covers your home address, which is why keeping your address current with USCIS matters more after a transfer than before it. USCIS's field office locator, on its own site, finds the office for a ZIP code.

People also report transfers to an office that isn't their local one. USCIS doesn't explain each one, and an office far from home usually means it was given the case to spread the work between offices. If that happens, the interview notice names the office and its address, and the [I-485 by field office](/i485-by-field-office) list shows how that office's cases have moved.

Not every case moves. Many employment-based I-485s are approved without a transfer or an interview, straight from the office that first handled them.

## How long it takes

The move came <ReportedMedianNow step="i485-office" /> after the I-485 was filed. After the move, the green card came <ReportedMedianNow step="office-gc" />. For the people the new office interviewed, the approval followed <ReportedMedianNow step="interview-gc" /> after the interview.

Offices differ a lot. San Fernando Valley, Los Angeles, Santa Ana and dozens of others have their own figures from the cases people reported there, beside USCIS's own counts. Find yours on the [I-485 by field office](/i485-by-field-office) list.

## What to do after a transfer

Nothing is due from you because of the move itself. Keep your address current with USCIS, watch for an interview notice by mail, and keep the job offer and the [medical exam](/guides/i693-medical-exam) current. The [I-485 timeline](/guides/i485-timeline-each-step) shows every step from filing, and the [interview guide](/guides/i485-interview-to-green-card) covers the wait after an interview.

## What these figures can't tell you

They come from people who chose to report their case, so they aren't a sample of everyone, and nobody can check a date people report after PERM. A transfer date people give is usually the day their online case status changed. Read each median as what the people who reported saw. If your case moved, add your dates on the [green card timelines](/green-card-timelines) page: it takes a minute and needs no account.
