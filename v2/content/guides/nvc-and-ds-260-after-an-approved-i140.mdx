When your employer's I-140 is approved and you live outside the US, your green card comes as an immigrant visa from a US consulate. The State Department's National Visa Center, or NVC, prepares the case first. PERM Tracker's free [consulates page](/consular-posts) shows how far each consulate has got with scheduling, from State's own scheduling table, beside the immigrant visas that post issued.

<Callout type="important" title="The short answer">
USCIS sends the approved I-140 to NVC. NVC sends you a Welcome Letter. You pay the fees, fill in Form DS-260 for each person immigrating, and upload your documents. Once your case is complete and a visa number is available for your priority date, a consulate interviews you. A visa in your passport then lets you travel and ask to be admitted as a permanent resident.
</Callout>

## Where the steps come from

The steps here come from three official sources, each read on November 25, 2026:

- USCIS's [Consular Processing](https://www.uscis.gov/green-card/green-card-processes-and-procedures/consular-processing) page (last reviewed July 20, 2023)
- The State Department's own manual for consular officers, the Foreign Affairs Manual, at [9 FAM 504](https://fam.state.gov/fam/09FAM/09FAM050401.html)
- The State Department's visa rules in Title 22 of the Code of Federal Regulations, [part 42](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-22/chapter-I/subchapter-F/part-42) on eCFR

State's step-by-step pages on travel.state.gov refuse automated readers. Where this guide draws on them, it read the Internet Archive's copies of those pages from January 2026, and it says so.

## Before NVC: choosing a consulate on the I-140

The choice between a consulate and a green card application inside the US is made on the I-140 itself. Part 4 of Form I-140 asks whether the worker will apply for a visa at a US embassy or consulate abroad, and which city, or will apply inside the US to adjust status ([Form I-140](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/forms/i-140.pdf), edition 06/07/24).

USCIS says that once it approves the petition for someone living outside the US, or for someone in the US who wants to apply abroad, it sends the approved petition to NVC. The petition stays there until a visa number is available ([Consular Processing](https://www.uscis.gov/green-card/green-card-processes-and-procedures/consular-processing)). [I-140 processing times and premium processing](/guides/i140-processing-times-and-premium) covers the wait before that approval.

## The NVC steps, in order

NVC's part is to collect your fees, your application and your documents, then hand a complete case to the consulate. This is the order State's manual and its own pages describe:

<StepByStep>
<Step number={1} title="NVC creates the case and sends a Welcome Letter">
When NVC receives an approved petition, it sends the NVC Welcome Letter to everyone on the case, by email or by post when there's no email address. That's in State's manual at 9 FAM 504.4-2(B), updated December 30, 2024. State's NVC page, archived January 2026, says the letter gives you what you need to log in to CEAC, its Consular Electronic Application Center. That's where you check the case, read messages and manage it.
</Step>
<Step number={2} title="Pay the fees in CEAC">
There are two: the immigrant visa application fee for each applicant, and an affidavit of support fee for the cases that need one. State's fee page, as archived in January 2026, says CEAC takes them one at a time from a US bank account. PERM Tracker's [green card fees calculator](/tools/green-card-fees) shows the amounts.
</Step>
<Step number={3} title="Fill in Form DS-260 for each person immigrating">
Form DS-260 is the online immigrant visa application. You, and each family member immigrating with you, fill one in once CEAC shows the fees as paid. The form is required and can't be waived (22 CFR 42.63(a)(1)). Print the confirmation page and bring it to the interview (State's DS-260 page, archived January 2026).
</Step>
<Step number={4} title="Gather and upload the documents">
The rule asks, where obtainable, for police certificates, a certified birth record, any prison or military record, and records that show your marriage and children (22 CFR 42.65(b) and (c)). You scan them and upload them to CEAC.
</Step>
<Step number={5} title="NVC checks that the case is complete">
State's manual calls an NVC case documentarily complete once four things are in. Every fee is paid and every traveling applicant has a DS-260. The affidavit of support is in where one is required, and so are the police certificates (9 FAM 504.1-2, updated November 9, 2023).
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## The affidavit of support, needed only when a relative is involved

An employment case needs Form I-864, the affidavit of support, in one situation only. A relative who's a US citizen or permanent resident filed the petition, or owns a significant share of the company that filed it. A brother or sister counts only if a citizen (8 CFR 213a.2(a)(2)(i)(C), read on [eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-213a/section-213a.2) on November 25, 2026).

If CEAC bills you for the affidavit of support fee and your case doesn't need one, State's fee page, as archived in January 2026, says to contact NVC before paying it.

## When NVC asks, and when the consulate interviews

Two dates in the visa bulletin decide the timing, and they do different jobs. NVC sends the instructions to gather documents once your priority date is within the bulletin's Dates for Filing chart (9 FAM 504.4-2(B)). An interview comes only once a visa number is allocated, and State's manual tells posts not to schedule numbered cases before then (9 FAM 504.4-2(D), updated April 5, 2023).

Numbers are allocated each month from the count of documentarily complete applicants. When more people are waiting than numbers exist, the date of the first applicant who can't get one becomes the final action date (9 FAM 504.1-2). [How the visa bulletin works](/guides/how-the-visa-bulletin-works) explains the two charts, and PERM Tracker's [visa bulletin page](/visa-bulletin) shows this month's dates.

NVC schedules most interviews, generally in the order cases became documentarily complete, within the number of appointments each consulate gives it (9 FAM 504.1-2). So the wait for an interview can differ from one consulate to another. The [consulates page](/consular-posts) shows each post's scheduling month. If your consulate has postponed interviews, [the interview pause guide](/guides/immigrant-visa-interview-pause) covers what's known and where to watch.

## The medical exam and the interview

Every immigrant visa applicant has a medical exam before the visa is issued, with a doctor chosen from the consulate's approved panel (22 CFR 42.66). The appointment letter tells you how to prepare for it, what original documents to bring and what photos you need (9 FAM 504.4-2(D)).

Submitting the DS-260 online doesn't finish your application. At the interview the officer asks whether you stand by what's on it, makes any changes you need, and you sign it under oath with a fingerprint (22 CFR 42.67(a)(3)). The decision to issue or refuse rests on that personal interview (9 FAM 504.1-3, updated March 26, 2025).

## After the interview

If the visa is approved, it's placed in your passport, and it lasts up to six months from the day it's issued (22 CFR 42.72(a)). State's page, as archived in January 2026, says it can be shorter when the medical exam expires sooner. The same page warns against selling your home, quitting your job or booking non-refundable travel until you actually have the visa.

Before you travel, pay the USCIS Immigrant Fee. USCIS uses it to process your visa packet and make your Green Card, and it won't mail the card until the fee is paid. You hand the visa packet, unopened, to the border officer, who decides whether to admit you. If the card hasn't come within 90 days of arriving, USCIS asks you to contact it ([Consular Processing](https://www.uscis.gov/green-card/green-card-processes-and-procedures/consular-processing)).

When the officer doesn't have enough to decide the case, the visa is refused under section 221(g) of the immigration law. A missing document is one example. Some of those cases then go into administrative processing. Outside an emergency, State's page asks you to wait at least 180 days before asking about administrative processing. The count runs from the interview or from your last documents, whichever is later (archived January 2026).

## The one-year rule

Your registration with NVC ends if you don't apply for the visa within one year after State tells you a visa is available. It can be reinstated if the delay was beyond your control, such as an illness that kept you from traveling. You have to show that before the end of the second year (22 CFR 42.83). A registration ends the same way one year after a 221(g) refusal if you send nothing to overcome it.

USCIS also asks you to tell NVC about three changes: a move, a child turning 21 and a change in marital status. Each can affect eligibility ([Consular Processing](https://www.uscis.gov/green-card/green-card-processes-and-procedures/consular-processing)). The [CSPA age calculator](/tools/cspa-age-calculator) checks whether a child could age out.

## What this can't tell you

- When your own interview will be. That depends on your priority date, your consulate's capacity and how many complete cases are ahead of yours.
- The fees on the day you pay. They change, so the [fees calculator](/tools/green-card-fees) and CEAC are where to read them.
- What State's travel.state.gov pages say today. What this guide takes from them comes from January 2026 copies, and the pages may have changed since.
- Anything about a green card applied for inside the US. That's the I-485, covered in [the I-485 timeline](/guides/i485-timeline-each-step).
