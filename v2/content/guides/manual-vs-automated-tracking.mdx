For immigration attorneys managing fewer than 5-10 active PERM cases, a well-maintained spreadsheet can work effectively. Beyond 10 cases, purpose-built automated tracking tools like PERM Tracker's free [case-management app](/for-attorneys) (which also syncs each case's deadlines to Google Calendar), LawLogix, or Docketwise significantly reduce the risk of missed deadlines by automatically calculating dates from PERM regulations and sending proactive notifications. The best approach for most practices is a hybrid: automated tools for deadline calculation and alerts, plus spreadsheets for custom reporting.

## The tracking challenge

Every immigration attorney managing [PERM labor certification](https://flag.dol.gov/programs/perm) cases needs a system for tracking deadlines, recruitment activities, and case status. Every practice tracks these. The difference is how.

<Callout type="stat" stat="50+ dates" title="Dates to track in one case">
  A single PERM case generates 50+ interconnected deadlines across its lifecycle under [20 C.F.R. pt. 656 (2025)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-V/part-656). At 10+ active cases, manual tracking becomes a liability.
</Callout>

Spreadsheets are a common start, and they strain as a caseload grows. This guide compares the two main approaches, including where manual tracking makes more sense. For a strategic overview of the PERM process itself, see [the ultimate PERM guide](/guides/ultimate-perm-guide-2026).

## Manual tracking (spreadsheets)

Manual tracking means a spreadsheet you build yourself, with a column for each deadline, dates worked out by hand and calendar reminders set one by one. It's free and fully under your control, but it gets unwieldy beyond 10 to 15 active cases.

### How it works

Most manual systems involve:
- An Excel or Google Sheets workbook with columns for each deadline
- Color-coded cells for urgency
- Manual calculation of deadlines from key dates
- Calendar reminders set individually for each deadline

### Pros

- **Full control**: You define every column, formula, and process
- **No learning curve**: Spreadsheets are familiar tools
- **No dependencies**: Works offline, no subscription, no vendor risk
- **Free**: No software cost
- **Customizable**: Adapt to your specific workflow

### Cons

- **Manual calculation errors**: One wrong formula can cascade through dates
- **No automatic notifications**: Must manually set calendar reminders
- **Scaling issues**: Gets unwieldy beyond 10-15 active cases
- **No validation**: Won’t catch regulatory compliance issues
- **Single point of failure**: A corrupted spreadsheet can take every date with it
- **Collaboration challenges**: Difficult to share with team members

If the dates have to live in a spreadsheet or a CRM such as Salesforce, the arithmetic doesn't have to be done by hand. The [PERM deadline calculator](/tools/perm-deadline-calculator) works out every date from the wage determination and the recruitment steps, copies them all at once to paste in, and makes a calendar file with a reminder a week before each one. It runs the same date rules as PERM Tracker itself.

## Automated tracking tools

Automated tools such as PERM Tracker, LawLogix and Docketwise work out deadlines from the dates you enter and send alerts before each one. The trade-off is relying on outside software and learning a new system.

### How they work

Purpose-built tools like PERM Tracker, LawLogix, and Docketwise automate the calculation and notification aspects of case management. PERM Tracker also reads DOL's own case status for a number you give it, so the status side isn't manual either. They typically provide:

- Automatic deadline calculation from entered dates
- Push/email notifications for upcoming deadlines
- Visual dashboards and calendar views
- Recruitment tracking checklists
- Validation against PERM regulations

### Pros

- **Automated deadlines**: Calculated from the regulations rather than hand-built formulas
- **Notifications**: Proactive alerts before deadlines
- **Validation**: Catches compliance issues before filing
- **Scalability**: Handles any caseload size
- **Visual tracking**: Dashboards, calendars, timelines

### Cons

- **Vendor dependency**: Reliance on external software
- **Learning curve**: Time to learn a new system
- **Feature gaps**: May not cover your exact workflow
- **Data migration**: Moving existing cases into a new system

## When each approach makes sense

A spreadsheet makes sense for fewer than 5 to 10 active PERM cases. Automation makes sense from 10 active cases, or once a deadline has been missed or nearly missed.

### Stick with spreadsheets when:

- You manage fewer than 5-10 active PERM cases
- You’re a solo practitioner with a small, manageable caseload
- You have a proven system that hasn’t caused issues
- You need highly customized tracking that no tool supports

### Switch to automation when:

- You’re managing 10+ active cases simultaneously
- You’ve missed or nearly missed a deadline
- You’re spending significant time on manual calculations
- You’re adding team members who need access
- You want proactive notifications instead of reactive calendar checks

## The hybrid approach

Many practices use both: a tracking tool for deadline automation and notifications, plus a spreadsheet for custom metrics, reporting, or case-specific notes that don’t fit neatly into any tool.

The key is identifying which parts of your workflow benefit most from automation (deadline calculation, notifications, validation) and which benefit from flexibility (custom reporting, unusual case types).

## Choosing for your practice

There’s no single answer for every practice. The best approach depends on your caseload size, team structure and existing systems. Either way, you need a reliable system that ensures no deadline is missed, whether it’s a well-maintained spreadsheet or a purpose-built tool.
