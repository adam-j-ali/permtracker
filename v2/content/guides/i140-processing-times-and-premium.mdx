The I-140 is the petition an employer files with USCIS after DOL certifies the PERM application. Without premium processing it has no deadline at all. With it, USCIS has to act within 15 or 45 business days. PERM Tracker's free [I-140 wait calculator](/tools/i140-calculator) counts the day USCIS must act on a premium petition, and puts USCIS's published processing time beside the number of petitions still waiting in each category.

<Callout type="important" title="The short answer">
A regular I-140 waits its turn, and the figures for your category are on the pages linked here. Premium processing makes USCIS act within 15 business days, or 45 for multinational executives and managers and national interest waivers. Acting can mean an approval, a denial or a request for more evidence, and an evidence request starts the count again.
</Callout>

## When the I-140 is filed

The I-140 goes in after the labor certification is approved, and USCIS has to receive it within 180 days of that certification. USCIS's [I-140 page](https://www.uscis.gov/i-140) says a petition with an expired labor certification is rejected (last reviewed July 24, 2026).

It can be filed by mail or online. There's one catch for premium processing. An I-140 filed together with Form I-907, the premium request, has to go by mail. A pending I-140 filed online, with a receipt number starting IOE, can take a standalone I-907 through the online account or by mail (the same page).

[The ETA 9089 expiration guide](/guides/eta-9089-expiration-and-i140-deadline) works out the 180-day date from a certification. The priority date that decides your place in the visa line doesn't come from the I-140 at all. When a labor certification comes with the petition, the priority date is the day DOL accepted the PERM application for processing (8 CFR 204.5(d) on [eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-204/subpart-A/section-204.5), read November 25, 2026).

## How long a regular I-140 takes

A regular I-140 has no clock, because USCIS sets no date for deciding it. The wait depends on how many petitions are pending in your category and how fast USCIS is getting through them. USCIS's glossary calls a processing time an estimate that varies from case to case ([USCIS glossary](https://www.uscis.gov/tools/glossary), read November 25, 2026).

The numbers move, so they live on pages that update rather than in this guide:

- The [I-140 wait calculator](/tools/i140-calculator) shows each category's pending count beside the time USCIS posts, and says when the two disagree.
- The [I-140 processing times page](/uscis-processing-times/i-140) shows USCIS's quarterly median for the form, dated by the quarter it describes.
- [I-140 trends](/tools/i140-trends) charts petitions received, approved and denied by quarter.

USCIS publishes two different times for one form, and they can be months apart. [Median vs 80%](/guides/uscis-processing-times-median-vs-80-percent) explains which one answers which question.

## How to read USCIS's processing times page

Pick your form, its category and the office, and read the time it shows. Then enter your receipt date to get your case inquiry date. The inquiry date is the one that tells you when USCIS will take a question about your case.

These steps are the CIS Ombudsman's, from its alert of May 16, 2024 ([Case Assistance Alerts](https://www.dhs.gov/case-assistance-alerts), last updated March 11, 2025). The Ombudsman is an independent office in the Department of Homeland Security, outside USCIS. USCIS's own processing times page couldn't be read for this guide.

<StepByStep>
<Step number={1} title="Find your receipt date">
It's printed on the receipt notice USCIS sent you, the I-797C.
</Step>
<Step number={2} title="Choose the form, the category and the office">
On USCIS's Check Case Processing Times page, choose Form I-140 from the first menu, then the form category, then the office. Select Get processing time.
</Step>
<Step number={3} title="Read the processing time for what it is">
The figure labeled processing time is how long USCIS took to finish 80% of the cases it decided in the past six months. The Ombudsman's alert says it doesn't show how soon USCIS will act on your case.
</Step>
<Step number={4} title="Get your inquiry date">
Scroll to the question about when you can ask about your case, enter your receipt date and select Get Inquiry Date. If USCIS says your case is processing normally, it shows the earliest day you can ask, and that day can change. If it offers a service request link instead, you can ask now.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

PERM Tracker's [USCIS case inquiry date tool](/tools/uscis-case-inquiry) puts both of USCIS's figures on one calendar for your receipt date, with the inquiry day marked.

## Premium processing: the 15 and 45 business-day clocks

Premium processing is a paid request, on Form I-907, that makes USCIS act on an I-140 within 15 business days, or within 45 for two classifications. If USCIS misses the deadline, it refunds the fee and keeps working on the case. The rule is 8 CFR 106.4, read on [eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-106/section-106.4) on November 25, 2026, and USCIS's page is [How Do I Request Premium Processing?](https://www.uscis.gov/forms/all-forms/how-do-i-request-premium-processing) (last reviewed November 10, 2026).

| I-140 classification | USCIS's code | USCIS acts within |
|---|---|---|
| Extraordinary ability | E11 | 15 business days |
| Outstanding professors and researchers | E12 | 15 business days |
| Advanced degree or exceptional ability, no national interest waiver | E21 | 15 business days |
| Skilled workers | E31 | 15 business days |
| Professionals | E32 | 15 business days |
| Other workers | EW3 | 15 business days |
| Multinational executives and managers | E13 | 45 business days |
| National interest waiver | E21 | 45 business days |

Each part of the clock has its own rule:

- Acting means USCIS issues an approval, a denial, a notice of intent to deny or a request for evidence (106.4(f)(1)). USCIS's page adds a fifth: opening an investigation for fraud or misrepresentation.
- For the 15-day classes, the count starts the day USCIS receives the form and the fee. For the two 45-day classes, it starts once USCIS has the form, the fee and everything else it needs to decide the case (106.4(f)(2)). So for those two, the start can come after the I-907 arrives.
- A business day is a day the federal government is open. Weekends and federal holidays don't count. Neither does a day offices close, whether for weather or any other reason. The closure can be nationwide or only where the case is decided (106.4(e)(22)).
- An evidence request stops the count. A new full period starts on the day USCIS receives your answer (106.4(f)(3)).
- If USCIS doesn't act in time, it refunds the fee and still decides the case (106.4(f)(4)). The exception is a fraud investigation, where USCIS may keep the fee without telling you (106.4(f)(5)).

The premium clock on the [I-140 wait calculator](/tools/i140-calculator) takes the day USCIS received your I-907, or your evidence response, and shows the business day USCIS must act by. It counts from the day you enter. It can't know about a closure that hasn't happened yet, and for a 45-day class you may need to enter a later start, for the reason above.

## Who files it, and how

Only the employer, or the attorney or representative who filed for it, files the I-907 for an employer's I-140. The worker may pay the fee but can't sign or file the form, unless the worker is also the petitioner, as in a self-petition (USCIS's premium processing page).

To upgrade a pending I-140, the I-907 goes to the service center where the I-140 is pending, with a copy of the I-140's receipt notice. A standalone I-907 sent to the wrong service center isn't rejected, but its clock starts only when the right one receives it. One sent to a lockbox is rejected. Send one I-907 per I-140, and keep I-907s for other petitions out of that envelope (the same page).

In a green card case, premium processing reaches the I-140 and nothing after it. USCIS's list of forms that offer it names the I-129, the I-140, certain I-765s and certain I-539s, and the I-485 isn't among them. So paying for a faster I-140 doesn't speed up an I-485 filed with it. The fee can't be waived ([106.4(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-106/section-106.4)), and it can be adjusted every two years in line with consumer prices (106.4(d)). PERM Tracker's [green card fees calculator](/tools/green-card-fees) shows the fee beside the rest of the fees.

USCIS can also switch premium processing off for a form when it can't keep up (106.4(g)(2)). It has done that for the I-140 before. Its page says it suspended I-140 premium processing on July 2, 2007 and brought it back on June 29, 2009.

## When premium is worth it

Premium processing gets you the first decision sooner, and that's all it buys. It doesn't move your priority date, which was fixed when DOL accepted the PERM application, and it doesn't shorten the wait for a visa number.

It can matter when a date depends on the approval itself. The [I-140 wait calculator](/tools/i140-calculator) lists three such cases with the rule behind each: an H-1B near its six-year limit, a worker who might change jobs, and a priority date that's about to become current. If your date is years from current, a faster approval changes little. [Approved I-140, no visa number](/guides/approved-i140-no-visa-number-eb2-india) shows what that wait looks like.

## What this can't tell you

- When your own regular I-140 will be decided. USCIS publishes how long past cases took. It doesn't publish where any one petition sits in line.
- Whether USCIS will approve. Premium buys a decision by a date, and that decision can be a request for evidence or a denial.
- Whether premium is open for your classification today. USCIS can suspend it, so check its page before filing.
- The fee today. It's on USCIS's fee schedule and the [fees calculator](/tools/green-card-fees).
