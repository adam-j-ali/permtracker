The five most common PERM audit triggers are: (1) unusual or tailored job requirements that exceed the occupation norm, (2) foreign language requirements without clear business necessity, (3) related employer-beneficiary relationships, (4) recruitment documentation gaps, and (5) timing violations in the recruitment process. DOL publishes no audit rate, so nobody outside the Department knows the share. Some audits are random quality-control selections rather than a finding about the case. PERM Tracker's [RFI and audit page](/perm-rfi-audit) counts the PERM cases at each review stage today, read case by case from DOL's own status record, pending cases included.

## Understanding PERM audits

A PERM audit occurs when the [Department of Labor](https://flag.dol.gov/programs/perm) selects an application for additional review. Audited cases leave the ordinary queue for a separate, slower one. Until October 31, 2026, [OFLC's processing times page](https://flag.dol.gov/processingtimes) printed the filing month each queue had reached, so the cost of an audit in months could be read off it. It now prints only the average days to an analyst-review decision, and no audit figure at all. The audit itself requires the employer to submit supporting documentation under the procedures at [20 C.F.R. § 656.20 (2025)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-V/part-656/subpart-C/section-656.20).

## Trigger 1: Unusual job requirements

The DOL scrutinizes job requirements that appear tailored to a specific candidate. If the minimum requirements exceed what’s normally required for similar positions, expect an audit.

**Common red flags:**
- Requiring specific technologies that are uncommon in the field
- Experience requirements exceeding the SVP level for the occupation
- Dual degree requirements without clear business justification
- Combinations of skills that are unusually specific

**How to avoid it:**
- Keep requirements within the [O*NET](https://www.onetonline.org/) job zone for the occupation
- Document the business necessity for any above-normal requirements per [20 C.F.R. § 656.17(h)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-V/part-656/subpart-C/section-656.17)
- Review comparable job postings to benchmark requirements

## Trigger 2: Foreign language requirements

Requiring fluency in a language other than English almost always triggers an audit. The DOL wants to be sure it isn’t a way to limit the applicant pool.

**How to avoid it:**
- Only require foreign language skills when the business needs them
- Prepare detailed business necessity documentation
- Document the percentage of clients or business conducted in the language
- Consider whether the requirement can be listed as preferred rather than required

## Trigger 3: Related employer-beneficiary relationships

When the beneficiary has a familial, ownership, or other close relationship with the employer, the DOL will want to verify that the recruitment was in good faith.

**Common scenarios:**
- Family-owned businesses sponsoring a family member
- Beneficiary who’s a partial owner of the company
- Small businesses where the beneficiary has a central role

**How to avoid it:**
- Conduct thorough, well-documented recruitment
- Maintain detailed records of all applicants and interview notes
- Run the recruitment in good faith, open to U.S. workers

## Trigger 4: Recruitment documentation gaps

Inadequate or inconsistent recruitment documentation is a frequent audit trigger. The DOL expects complete records of every recruitment step.

**What the DOL looks for:**
- Proof of all required recruitment activities
- Complete records of applicant responses
- Documentation of why U.S. applicants were rejected (if any)
- Consistency between the job posting and the ETA 9089

**How to avoid it:**
- Create a recruitment file checklist before starting
- Save copies of all advertisements, job orders, and postings
- Document every applicant interaction with dates and outcomes
- Keep job descriptions consistent across all recruitment materials

<Callout type="tip" title="Tip">
Use a tracking system that timestamps and organizes your recruitment documentation. It makes an audit response much easier.
</Callout>

## Trigger 5: Timing issues

The DOL pays close attention to the timing of recruitment activities under [20 C.F.R. § 656.17(e)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-V/part-656/subpart-C/section-656.17). Filing too early or too late relative to recruitment activities can trigger an audit.

<Callout type="stat" stat="30 days" title="The audit response window">
  When the DOL issues an audit letter, you have **30 calendar days** to respond with all requested documentation. This deadline is strict. Build your audit file from day one, well before any letter arrives.
</Callout>

**Key timing rules:**
- The SWA job order and the two Sunday ads must run at least 30 days, and no more than 180 days, before filing
- No recruitment step may fall more than 180 days before filing
- Only one of the three additional steps may fall entirely within the last 30 days before filing
- The Notice of Filing goes up between 30 and 180 days before filing, for at least 10 consecutive business days ([20 C.F.R. § 656.10(d)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-V/part-656/subpart-C/section-656.10))

**How to avoid it:**
- Create a detailed timeline before starting recruitment. Our [recruitment checklist](/guides/perm-recruitment-checklist) covers every timing requirement
- Track all dates carefully using [dedicated tracking tools](/guides/recruitment-tracking)
- Build in buffer time between recruitment end and filing

## Getting ready for an audit

Even with careful preparation, audits can happen. These habits make one easier to answer:

1. **Maintain complete files**: Keep copies of everything from day one
2. **Respond promptly**: You typically have 30 days to respond to an audit
3. **Be thorough**: Err on the side of more documentation than required
4. **Track deadlines**: Missing an audit response deadline results in automatic denial
