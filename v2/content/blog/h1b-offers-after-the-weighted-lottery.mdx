The H-1B lottery used to be a random draw. For the fiscal 2027 cap it was weighted by pay for the first time, and the Department of Labor's own filings now show what came out the other side. In the three months after the draw, the middle offer on an H-1B wage filing was $122,000. A year earlier it was $108,000. PERM Tracker's [H-1B salary explorer](/lca-wages) reads every one of those filings DOL has published since fiscal 2020, so you can check any job, state or year yourself.

## The middle offer rose 13% in one spring

Before an employer can file an H-1B petition, it files a labor condition application (an LCA) with DOL. The LCA names the job, the place and the pay. DOL publishes every one.

This counts the LCAs that DOL certified in April, May and June, the months right after the lottery, when employers file for the people who were picked.

| April to June | Certified filings | Middle offer | Under $100,000 | $150,000 or more |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2020 | 181,122 | $92,000 | 60.6% | 11.1% |
| 2021 | 182,635 | $95,000 | 55.1% | 13.5% |
| 2022 | 220,527 | $99,800 | 50.4% | 17.4% |
| 2023 | 170,460 | $100,000 | 48.9% | 17.4% |
| 2024 | 200,730 | $102,700 | 44.6% | 19.3% |
| 2025 | 220,336 | $108,000 | 41.1% | 22.8% |
| 2026 | 215,840 | $122,000 | 30.7% | 31.8% |

From 2020 to 2025 the middle offer never moved more than 5.2% from one spring to the next. In 2026 it moved 13.0%. The number of filings barely changed.

## Fewer filings at the lowest wage level

DOL sorts every job's pay into four wage levels for its occupation and area. Level I is the entry level and Level IV is the highest. Each LCA says which level its wage was set against.

Among spring filings for new jobs, which is where lottery winners show up:

| April to June, new jobs | Filings | Level I | Level II | Level III | Level IV | No level |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 | 79,330 | 29.0% | 50.3% | 11.2% | 5.7% | 3.8% |
| 2024 | 110,037 | 33.0% | 45.5% | 10.7% | 5.4% | 5.4% |
| 2025 | 120,471 | 38.1% | 39.1% | 10.6% | 6.4% | 5.8% |
| 2026 | 114,145 | 26.6% | 35.1% | 15.2% | 14.1% | 9.0% |

Level I had grown three springs in a row. In 2026 it fell by 11.5 points. Level IV had stayed between 4.9% and 6.4% in every spring from 2020 to 2025, and it reached 14.1%.

## Software developers moved the same way

Software developers are the largest H-1B occupation. Their middle spring offer was $127,500 in 2025 and $143,000 in 2026, on about the same number of filings (60,684 and 58,664). The share under $100,000 was 39.8% in spring 2023 and 19.3% in spring 2025. In spring 2026 it was 7.8%.

Every other occupation taken together moved too: from $100,000 in spring 2025 to $112,400 in spring 2026.

## Why it moved, and what the filings can't say

The lottery's own rule changed between the two springs. DHS's weighted selection rule took effect on February 27, 2026, in time for the fiscal 2027 cap ([90 FR 60864](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/12/29/2025-23853/weighted-selection-process-for-registrants-and-petitioners-seeking-to-file-cap-subject-h-1b)). A registration now goes into the draw once at Level I, twice at Level II, three times at Level III and four times at Level IV. DHS estimated a Level I worker's chance at about 15% and a Level IV worker's at about 61%, against about 30% for everyone under the old random draw.

The winter quarter is the comparison. In January to March 2026, before the draw, the middle offer was 1.2% above the same quarter of 2025. The change arrives in the quarter the lottery feeds.

Three limits are worth knowing.

- The filings can't tell a raised offer from a different person. A draw that favors higher wage levels picks higher-paid people even if no employer changes what it pays.
- An LCA isn't a worker or a petition. One LCA can cover several workers, employers file some they never use, and spring filings for new jobs also include jobs outside the cap.
- Something else changed too. A $100,000 payment on certain new H-1B petitions began in September 2025, and a federal court set aside the guidance for collecting it on June 8, 2026. DOL's file can't separate its effect from the lottery's. [Rule changes since August](/blog/rule-changes-since-august-2026) follows where that payment stands.

## How this was counted

Certified LCAs from DOL's quarterly disclosure files, grouped by the date DOL decided them, through June 30, 2026. They cover three programs that share the form: H-1B, H-1B1 and E-3. Pay by the hour, week or month is turned into a yearly figure. A wage that can't be pay for its period is left out, as in the salary explorer. "Middle offer" is the median, to the nearest $100. "New jobs" are filings that name at least one position as new employment. Each year's totals were checked against the salary explorer's own yearly figures, and they matched.

Source: PERM Tracker (permtracker.app), from the Department of Labor's LCA disclosure files, fiscal 2020 through the third quarter of fiscal 2026.
