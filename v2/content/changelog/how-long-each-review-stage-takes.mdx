People waiting on a reconsideration, an RFI or a hold ask how long it takes. DOL publishes no time for these steps, and since October 31, 2026, it no longer names the month of reconsideration requests it's working on. Each step's page under [RFIs, audits and appeals](/perm-rfi-audit) now answers from our own daily record, starting with [reconsideration appeals](/perm-rfi-audit/reconsideration-appeals).

## What each stage page shows

- How long the cases waiting there have waited so far, as a chart and as a typical figure. A wait starts on the day our daily check saw the case arrive. For an appeal that began before our record did, it starts on DOL's own denial date from its quarterly file.
- Every case that has left the stage since August 30, 2026, newest first: where it went, the day, and how long it had waited. Search it, sort it, filter it by outcome, or download it as CSV.
- A weekly chart of the cases leaving the stage, split by where they went.
- On the reconsideration page, the months DOL posted before it dropped the line, and how many of the requests we saw arrive have had an answer.
- Questions people ask about the step, answered from the same figures.

## Fixed

The figure that read "Typical wait so far" counted days since the PERM was filed, not days at the step. On the reconsideration page it said 528 days, when the typical case we can date had waited 67 days. It's replaced by the time at the step.

## Where it shows up

- A case lookup at one of these statuses links to its stage page.
- A case alert for a case at one of these statuses links there too. A denial's alert links to how long reconsideration is taking.
- A certification's alert, and our congratulations note after one, invite you to add your dates to the [green card timelines](/green-card-timelines) as your I-140 and I-485 move.
