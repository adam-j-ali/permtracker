This release puts first things first on the case lists: the search, a short summary, then the cases. It also answers a question people ask about every estimate, which is whether it counts the holidays.

## Case lists start with the search

- The [wage request](/pwd-cases), [H-1B LCA](/lca-cases), [seasonal](/seasonal-cases) and [PERM](/perm-cases) lists now open the same way: a search box, three figures, then the cases. Before, a queue chart or a set of filters came first, and the list began several screens down.
- On the wage, LCA and seasonal lists, one box takes an employer's name or a case number. A case number opens that case's page.
- A page holds 25 cases on a wide screen and 10 on a phone, with Previous and Next under it. "Rows per page" under each list changes that to 10, 25, 50 or 100.
- On a phone each case is four short lines: the employer with its status, the job title, the case number with the wage, then the dates. The PERM list is less than half as long on a phone as it was.
- On the PERM list, the filters you don't need at first sit behind one "More filters" button. It says how many are on.
- Each list's three figures now carry a small picture: how many requests are still waiting, and whether last month's filings went up or down.
- [Case search](/case-search) follows the same order. The search form comes first, then how many filings each of the four programs holds, then an example of an answer. Once you search, your results come first.
- Case search's results come a page at a time too: 10 on a phone and 25 on a wide screen, with the same Previous, Next and "Rows per page". They used to come 100 at a time. The CSV download still carries the whole answer.

## Visa interview waits you can search

- The table of every consulate's wait on the [visa wait times](/visa-wait-times) page is now a list you can search, sort and page. Type a city to find its consulate, or press a column to put the longest waits first.
- It opens at the longest wait for a work visa and shows 25 consulates a page. Before, all 241 sat in one long scrolling box.
- On a phone it shows the consulate with its work-visa and student-visa waits. The visitor and crew columns are in the wide table and in the download.

## A page for each USCIS office

- Every USCIS field office and service center now has a page of its own, such as [Hartford](/i485-by-field-office/hartford-connecticut) or the [National Benefits Center](/i485-by-field-office/national-benefits-center). Each shows the employment-based I-485s that office received, approved, denied and still held in each of the last four quarters, from USCIS's own file.
- The page says where the office's pending pile stands among the others, and gives the one processing time USCIS posts for employment-based cases at field offices. USCIS publishes no wait for a single office, so the page doesn't invent one.
- [The I-485 by field office](/i485-by-field-office) now opens with the search and the table of every office, 25 to a page. An office's name opens its page.
- Type an office's name in the search box at the top of any page to go straight to it.

## Timelines people share, by office

- Each office's page now has a place for people's own timelines: the days from filing the I-485 to biometrics, the interview and the green card. USCIS publishes none of this for a single office, so it comes from people who share their dates, and every figure says so.
- Adding yours takes a minute, on your office's page or on [green card timelines](/green-card-timelines). It needs no account and no PERM case number, so people with an EB-1 or a waiver case can add theirs too.
- We look at each timeline before it shows or counts anywhere. The form tells you it's waiting.
- An office's list opens once 3 people from that office have shared, and the full list at 10 (it was 25). A median still needs 5 timelines.
- Your browser keeps the key to your timeline. An edit code opens the same timeline on another phone or computer.
- The green card timelines page measures two more steps: from filing the I-485 to biometrics, and to the interview.

## What DOL decided today, on one page

- [Today's decisions](/perm-updates) shows DOL's last working day on one screen: how many PERM cases it certified, denied and saw withdrawn, and how that compares with a usual day of the week.
- It says which filing dates DOL reached: where 8 in 10 of the day's certified cases were filed, the busiest filing day, and a bar for each filing month.
- The day comes as one line you can copy or share into a chat, with the page's address typed out.
- Every case DOL moved that day is listed under it, with the wage requests it answered. Pick a status to narrow the list.
- Each day has its own address, such as `/perm-updates/2026-11-10`. The homepage's count for a day opens that day.
- A day DOL is still working says so, with the time of our last check and the next one.
- We now check DOL's record a fourth time each day, at 8:10 PM Eastern, so the evening's decisions show the same night.

## Your case's page puts the email first

- On a pending case's page, the estimated date and the email box now come straight after the status, above the picture of the line. On a phone the box used to sit a screen down.
- The box says how often we check: every 5 minutes on weekdays.
- If you already asked for emails about a case from this browser, its page shows the day you asked where the form was, with a way to ask again.
- If you've looked a case up on three or more days, the box tells you how many. Both of these are kept in your browser only, and the [Privacy Policy](/privacy) says so.
- A certified case's page now offers the next thing to watch: an email when the visa bulletin moves your cutoff, with the case's priority date beside it.
- Under it, a certified case's page asks one question: whether you'd want tracking for the I-140 and the I-485 by receipt number. We're working on it, and a box on the form lets you hear when it opens.
- Before you type a number, the [case status](/perm-case-status) page shows three figures. They're drawn now. The month DOL is deciding stands out, and a bar under each month shows how much of it DOL has decided and how much still waits.
- The count of undecided cases has a bar too: the cases filed in the oldest open month or earlier, beside the cases filed since.
- Under a pending case's estimated date, the page now says about how many days are left and how much of the estimated wait has passed, with a bar. A reader asked for it. Both come from the same estimate as the date, so they move when it does.

## The wage levels tool reads the proposed wage rule

- DOL has proposed raising all four prevailing wage levels, and its final rule went to the White House for review on November 6, 2026. The final numbers aren't public yet.
- The [wage levels tool](/tools/wage-levels) now shows, for the job and area you pick, each level today beside what the proposal would make it. The new Level I is about today's Level II.
- It says "about" and "above" where that's all today's figures can support, and it says plainly that today's levels are the ones in force.
- On a phone, each level's yearly and hourly figure now sits beside the level's name with its bar underneath. Before, the longest bar pushed the figure past the edge of the box.

## Lists a page at a time, sized for your screen

- A list of a day's cases shows 10 to a page on a phone and 25 on a wider screen.
- Under each of those lists you can choose 10, 25, 50 or 100 rows a page. The choice stays in the address, so a shared link keeps it.
- The [sponsor finder](/sponsor-finder) shows 25 sponsors a page, and 10 on a phone, with the same choice. It showed 50, which was seventeen phone screens.
- The [H-1B employer ranking](/h1b-employers) is one list you page through. Before, ranks 26 to 100 sat behind a button that opened all 75 at once. The lower ranks on the [law firm rankings](/law-firm-rankings) and the [cap-exempt employers](/h1b-cap-exempt-employers) page the same way.
- The table of [visa refusals](/visa-refusals) by ground shows a page of grounds at a time.
- An employer search on the [wage request](/pwd-cases), [LCA](/lca-cases) and [seasonal](/seasonal-cases) lists comes a page at a time as well: 10 cases on a phone and 25 on a wider screen. A search used to print every case it found in one run, up to 200 in each of its two tables.
- On a phone, the [employer](/perm-employers) and [law firm](/perm-attorneys) lists now fit the screen. Their Filings and Approval columns used to start past the edge, so you had to scroll sideways to read a figure.
- Three more lists follow the rule. The published decisions and a day's changes on [decisions over time](/perm-decision-activity) show 10 rows a page on a phone, where they showed 50 and 25. The table on [filings by day](/filings-by-day) shows 10 days on a phone and has the rows choice it lacked.

## Guides open with the case box

- Every guide and blog post now has the case box under its title, as every other page does. It used to sit only after the last paragraph.

## The month DOL is deciding, where you can use it

- DOL's own page stopped printing the filing month it's working on October 31, 2026. [PERM processing times](/perm-processing-times) still measures that month every day from DOL's case-status service. The box that emails you when DOL reaches your month now sits right under it. It used to be most of the way down the page.
- The [queue page](/perm-queue) has the same box, under its three figures.
- The email that tells you DOL has reached your month now has a button to look up your own case. That page shows DOL's status for it and offers an email when the status changes. The current figures are still linked under the button, with a search by employer for anyone without a case number.
- The oldest filing month DOL still has open sits beside the month it's deciding.
- Under the month there's one dated line to copy, for when you quote it.
- The page's questions now include "Which month is DOL processing PERM applications from now?"
- If you keep a processing-times page of your own, you can paste this answer and its figures into it, and the box keeps itself current. The code is on the page itself, under the answer ("Copy the code"), and on its card under [embeds](/badges#embed-perm-processing-times).
- The pasted box fits the page it's on. In a phone-width article its frame grows taller, so no figure is cut off. Before, a phone showed about three quarters of it.
- Every box under [embeds](/badges#embeds) now has a link of its own, so you can send someone straight to one.
- The [API](/developers)'s queue answer carries the month too: `perm.decidingNow` is the filing month DOL is deciding by our daily check, with its date, and `perm.oldestOpenMonth` is the oldest month still open. DOL's own month in that answer has been empty since October 31.

## PERM decisions beside wage determinations

- The homepage and [decisions over time](/perm-decision-activity#dol-days) now put the week's PERM decisions beside the week's wage determinations. Each is set against the week before, and a federal holiday in the week is named.
- Decisions over time lists the last two weeks day by day, with both counts in each row. A day one of our two checks hasn't reached yet reads "not counted". It never reads as zero.
- The same page compares each of the last 12 months with [the same days a year earlier](/perm-decision-activity#by-month). The change is per workday, so a holiday or a month still running doesn't read as DOL slowing down.
- October 2025 gets no percent. DOL was shut that month, and thousands of decisions against a handful isn't a comparison.

## Wage requests beside PERM applications, on every employer's page

- An employer's page has a new table: for each year since 2023, the wage requests it sent DOL beside the PERM applications it filed. A green card through work starts with the wage request, and the PERM application follows months later.
- The newest of each is dated above the table, so you can see whether an employer is starting green cards now and whether it's filing them.
- For an employer that mostly hires nurses or physical therapists, the table says those cases skip the PERM application and go straight to USCIS.
- [Is your employer still filing PERM?](/blog/is-my-company-still-filing-perm) has a new section with the count behind it: of the wages DOL issued on green card requests it received in 2023, about half had been named by a PERM application it decided by June 30, 2026. Six big employers' requests and applications sit side by side there.

## Follow an employer, case by case

- Every employer's page has a Follow box. Until now it emailed only when DOL moved that employer's cases as a group. Tick "Every case too" and it also emails each time one of the employer's PERM cases changes status: certified, denied, a request for information, a hold, a withdrawal or an appeal.
- The email lists each case by its number, with the job title and filing month DOL holds for it, and each number opens that case's page. It comes once a day at most, on days something changed.
- It's made for the people who manage a company's cases, in HR or at a law firm. On an employer with fewer than 50 cases waiting the box starts ticked, because DOL rarely moves that few as a group.
- The confirmation email says which of the two you picked, and [your email preferences](/email-preferences) show it beside each employer you follow.

## Summary figures, drawn

- The rows of summary figures on a dozen pages each carry a small icon now, so you can tell a wait from a wage from a count at a glance. The pages: [consulates](/consular-posts), [visa issuances](/visa-issuances), [visa refusals](/visa-refusals), [layoff disclosures](/perm-layoff-disclosures), [fast-growing jobs](/bright-outlook-jobs), [cap-exempt employers](/h1b-cap-exempt-employers), the [estimate scorecard](/estimate-scorecard), [RFI and audits](/perm-rfi-audit), the [job code finder](/soc-codes), the [green card planner](/tools/green-card-timeline) and [tools](/tools).
- A figure that's a share has a bar under it. On layoff disclosures, the bar under "Certified after a yes" shows three outcomes in the site's usual colours: certified green, denied red and withdrawn purple.
- A figure that changed says how. Visa issuances are set against the same month a year earlier, and visa refusals against the fiscal year before.
- On your own case's page, the count of cases filed before yours stands out. A decided case's outcome carries its colour: green for certified, red for denied, purple for withdrawn.

## Fixed

- A ticked box on an alert form now shows its tick. Before, the product news and weekly digest boxes under every alert form looked the same ticked as empty.
- On [decisions over time](/perm-decision-activity), a date range over the newest ten weeks showed "All programs (0)" and no cases. A range now adds up each of its days.
- The same page's date box offered days back to October 2007, and every day before 2015 answered "Those decisions didn't load". Those days load now.
- A count no longer reads "(0)" while it's still loading.
- A day with no change on that page says our checks recorded none that day. It used to say nothing matched and suggest clearing filters that weren't on.
- Pick a date that neither DOL's files nor our own check covers, and the reason is open on the page. It was folded shut under "We hold nothing for these dates".
- On a laptop-width screen, the "250" rows-per-page button on that page sat partly under "Earlier day". Each button has its own room now, and the Program box shows its whole label.
- Pick a date before the oldest file on that page and it says where the record starts. It used to get the explanation written for the gap between DOL's files and our own daily check.
- The [wage request list](/pwd-cases) called its finished requests "issued". That count holds withdrawn and returned requests too, so the button and the line above it say "decided", as the other lists do.
- "Check my case" is back in the data menu on the [case status](/perm-case-status) page. There it takes you to the case box on the page.
- On a phone, the first and last months on the queue's month strip were cut off ("Aug '25" read "Au"). They read in full now.
- On a phone, when your case was filed the month after the one DOL is deciding, the strip printed the two months' names on top of each other. They sit side by side now.
- On a phone, "The wall in front of this case" pushed your own month's bar off the right edge when the wall was only a few months long. A short wall now fits the screen, and a long one opens at your month.
- On a phone, the [queue](/perm-queue)'s heading "Decided" broke across two lines. The table's columns are sized from what they hold now, and the bars are a third wider.
- A tool you paste into your own page now opens whole. Nine of the [embeds](/badges#embeds), the case lookup among them, opened with their foot cut off on a phone. Their code now gives the frame the height it needs at every width, and longer tools still open as a window that scrolls. If you pasted one before today, copy its code again to get the new frame.

- More links that stand alone on a line are full-size tap targets on a phone. They were 17 to 20 pixels tall.

  - The four programs on [case search](/case-search).
  - The status names on [RFI and audits](/perm-rfi-audit).
  - The employers on [under review](/perm-employers/under-review).
  - Every name in the ranked lists on a city, state, industry or country page.
- A case certified in DOL's live record, before its quarterly file names the decision, was told how many cases were "filed before this one". It now says the day our check first saw it certified.
- The calendar button in every date box is as tall as the box. It was a third of that, which is small for a thumb.
- Seven articles still said comments were open on two DHS proposals, [the OPT fees](/blog/opt-fee-proposal-2026) and [the end of the 60-day grace period](/guides/h1b-60-day-grace-period-proposal). Comments closed on November 9 and November 10, 2026, and the articles say so now. No final rule had been published for either by November 12, 2026.
- The [wage levels guide](/guides/salary-high-enough-perm-h1b) says where DOL's wage rule stands: the final rule went to the White House for review on November 6, 2026.
- [Policy changes](/policy-changes) missed that step for five days, because the government's own list misspells the rule's title. A rule we already follow is now read whatever the list calls it.
- An employer's batch of decisions is now dated by the day DOL worked on it. DOL posts some of an evening's decisions after midnight, and those carried the next morning's date, so a Tuesday batch read as Wednesday's. This affected the dates on an employer's page and in employer alert emails.
- In the [layoff notices](/layoffs), Wisconsin's notices are read from the state's new page. Seventeen of them had been listed twice, the older copy with stray characters in the company's name.
- The [PERM case status](/perm-case-status) page leads again with the filing month DOL is deciding now. The page took that month from DOL's own table, DOL stopped printing it on October 31, 2026, and the figure dropped off the page. It now comes from our daily check of DOL's case status.
- On a phone, the arrow at the top left of a data page stays on the screen as you scroll, so the data menu is always one tap away. From November 10, 2026 it had scrolled away with the page. It's narrower too, so it no longer covers the first letters of a line.
- Several things this release had hidden or moved are back where they were:
  - On [PERM cases](/perm-cases), the filters (state, occupation, outcome and year) are open again on a wide screen. On a phone they're one tap away. "Rows per page" is under the list, and 500 rows is a choice again.
  - A phone's row in that list shows the day DOL decided, and the law firm under the employer.
  - Job titles in the case lists show two lines on a phone. They'd been cut to one.
  - [Visa interview waits](/visa-wait-times) shows every visa's column on a phone, visitor visas included.
  - The A to Z row on a filing day's list is on a phone again, as one row you can swipe.
  - The wage request, LCA and seasonal lists say again that a case number gets a live answer from DOL and an email when it moves.
  - A case box shows an example case number under it.
  - "Decisions over time" is back in the menu's PERM group, under "Today's decisions".
- On [case search](/case-search), the search box comes before the count of filings.
- The [queue](/perm-queue) page says DOL stopped printing its own month on October 31, 2026, where a paragraph comparing the two had dropped out.
- On a filing month's page, such as [February 2026](/perm-queue/2026-02), the box for each employer letter holds its share at every screen width. On a narrow phone, and at two tablet widths, the share ran past the edge of its box.
- On the same page, a screen reader or a voice command now finds each day and each letter by the words shown on it. Their spoken names had left those words out.
- The [USCIS case inquiry date](/tools/uscis-case-inquiry) tool no longer dates an I-140 by a median that isn't its own. USCIS's quarterly median counts every I-140 category together, and the tool had put it on one category's calendar as "half of cases like yours", which for an advanced-degree petition fell after the day eight in ten are decided. The median is now a sentence about all I-140s, and a case's calendar never shows half decided after eight in ten.
- A watched wage request, H-1B LCA or seasonal case now shows the day DOL's status changed. Its page had kept the day we first saw the case.

## The estimate says what the holidays add

- A PERM estimate already walks DOL's calendar. DOL is closed on federal holidays, and it decides fewer cases around Thanksgiving and from December 21 to January 5.
- The estimate now says so in one line under the date, with the number of days it allows for them. It's on a [case's page](/perm-case-status), the [timeline calculator](/tools/perm-timeline-calculator) and the decision card in the app.
- The line appears only when the wait holds such days.

## Case tracking for legal teams

- The case form asks before you leave it with unsaved changes, whichever link you click. Before, only Back and refresh asked, and a click on the menu lost the work.
- You can drag a case card with a mouse, and it stays where you drop it. Dragging used to work by touch and keyboard only, and a dropped card could snap back.
- Editing a case no longer posts a "Job description updated" notice when the job description didn't change.
- The calendar opens with its title and your deadlines. The Google Calendar offer is one link beside the title.
- An empty calendar keeps its filters and offers the way back, such as "Show everything". Before, hiding the last case left a message and no control.
- On the cases list, the search comes before the status buttons.
- A case link that was cut short shows "Case not found" with a way back to your cases.
- A case closed overnight for a missed deadline now sends its push notification as well as its email.
- A reminder about an RFI or RFE due date is sent once. A second run could repeat it.
- The last setup step says only what's true about the sample case.

## The Privacy Policy and the Terms

- Both pages were updated on November 11, 2026 to describe more of what the product already does.
- The Privacy Policy's section on timelines you share now covers the two boxes this release adds (the USCIS office and a biometrics date), the review before a timeline shows, the edit code, and the new floors for the lists. Nothing else new is collected.
- The Privacy Policy now covers the web server's 14-day request log and the files you can upload to a case. It names three emails it hadn't, says what stays after an account is deleted, and says where our nightly backup is made.
- The fixes that came first are listed in [the release before this one](/changelog/the-answer-first-and-uscis-times-by-category).
