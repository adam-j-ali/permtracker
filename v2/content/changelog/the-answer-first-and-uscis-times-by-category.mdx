This release moves the answer and the case box to the top of the homepage. It also fills in USCIS's own posted times, one category at a time.

## The homepage opens with the answer

- The [homepage](/) now puts the month DOL is working on, with its chart, beside the case number box. On a phone the box comes right after the answer. Before, it sat a screen and a half down.
- Under the box, "Past PERM already?" links straight to [I-140 times](/uscis-processing-times/i-140), the [visa bulletin](/visa-bulletin) and [I-485 times](/uscis-processing-times/i-485).
- The four figures sit in one row under both. The count of wage requests waiting now opens the [prevailing wage queue calculator](/tools/pwd-calculator).
- The approval bar has a heading of its own: "PERM approval rate".
- On the road from PERM to a green card, the I-140 stop now prints the time within which USCIS decided half its I-140s in the newest quarter.

## USCIS's posted time, by category

- Each form's page, such as [I-140](/uscis-processing-times/i-140), now lists what USCIS's own processing-times page posts for each category of that form. That's the time within which 8 cases in 10 were decided, and the receipt day before which USCIS takes a question about a case.
- Where several offices post the same category, the row gives the quickest and the slowest.
- These pages refresh the day a new posting is copied.

## A deadline that lands on a weekend or holiday

- The [I-140 deadline guide](/guides/eta-9089-expiration-and-i140-deadline) now says what USCIS does when a certification's last day falls on a Saturday, a Sunday or a federal holiday. A paper petition is on time the next business day. One filed online gets no extra day.
- The [rules roundup](/blog/rule-changes-since-august-2026) names the policy alert of September 23, 2026 that set this. It took effect on October 23, 2026.

## Find a case from whatever you know

- The finder under every case number box now takes a range of dates, the job title and the law firm as well as the employer. [Open it](/perm-case-status), fill in what you know, and one box is enough. Before, it needed the employer and the exact day.
- One date looks at that day. Two dates look at every day between them, up to three months apart.
- A search by law firm lists decided cases only, because DOL names the firm at the decision. The finder says so when you use it.
- It works the same for PERM cases, wage requests, H-1B LCAs and the seasonal programs.
- The same search is in the [API](/developers) (`GET /v1/cases`), the MCP server (`find_case`) and the chat assistant.

## Filings that point at each other

- A few H-1B LCAs name the wage request their wage came from. [Look one up](/perm-case-status) and its page now prints that request in DOL's record, linked to the request's own page.

## Figures that hold up when something breaks

- A page whose main figures can't be read keeps its last good version. A smaller figure that can't be read is left out. Neither is ever shown as zero or as "none".
- Right after each release, the stored figures behind the PERM pages are rebuilt. Until that finishes, a page says a count isn't ready yet, so new wording never sits on figures built by an older rule.
- The H-1B employer rankings in the [API](/developers) answer 503 when they can't be read, so a program can tell an outage from an empty result.
