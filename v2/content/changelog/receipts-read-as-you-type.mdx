A USCIS receipt number now gets read the moment you start typing it, wherever you type it on this site. The I-140 calculator also works out the day USCIS has to act on a premium case.

## What changed

- The [USCIS receipt box](/uscis-case-status) reads a number as you type it. The first three letters name the office, and the next two the fiscal year. A strip under the box shows the number in its parts. It names the first character that can't belong, offers a one-tap fix when the letter O was typed for a zero, and tells an A-number apart from a receipt number.
- Every receipt you look up on that page is saved in your browser, with a name if you give it one, so it's one tap away next time. The list never leaves your browser.
- The site search takes a receipt number and opens its page.
- The PERM case box names a receipt's office and year before it sends you to the USCIS page. The I-140 form in your account reads a receipt number the same way as you type it, and never stops you saving.
- The [I-140 calculator](/tools/i140-calculator#premium-h) works out the day USCIS must act on a premium case: 15 business days for most classifications, 45 for EB-1C and national interest waivers. An answer to a request for evidence starts a new count, so you can put in the day USCIS received it.
- The [green card planner](/tools/green-card-timeline) measures the time to prepare an I-140 from people's own reports: a median of 38 days over 24 cases in the last six months. It used to assume 30 days.
- [Compare](/compare) now lists two sites that track USCIS receipt numbers.
- The [wage request calculator](/tools/pwd-calculator) dates a request by the day it was filed, the same way the [wage requests](/pwd-cases) page and the case page do: the requests still in process filed before it, over the pace DOL has been finishing them. Its old estimate divided DOL's month table by a rate that halved in October, and ran weeks late.

## Corrected

- An employer's approval rate on a [PERM case page](/perm-case-status) counted withdrawn cases as decided. It now counts certified and denied cases, as the employer's own page does. The [corrections log](/changelog/corrections) has the details.
- A field office's questions said no median of reported waits existed yet, while the same page showed one. Each answer now reads the same reports as the page's figures.
- The [green card line](/tools/green-card-line) took PERM to last 440 days for anyone whose I-140 came after June 30, 2026. DOL's newest quarter says 336, and a morning check now warns when that table falls behind DOL's files.
- [Compare](/compare) listed reading a receipt as you type as something another tracker does and this site doesn't. This site does it too now.
- A receipt number pasted with an en dash or dots passed the box but not the results page. Both read it now.
- The wage request calculator's questions said no estimated wait was shown, while the page showed one. The answer now says how the estimate is worked out.
- On the pages about each step, the box beside people's reported cases called every figure the agency's. The PERM, wage request and LCA figures are our own daily count of DOL's records, and the box now says so.
